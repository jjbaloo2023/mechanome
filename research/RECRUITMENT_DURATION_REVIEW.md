# Independent review: selected-observation duration null

Task `recruitment-duration-review-001`, 2026-09-29. Reviewed [the theory note](RECRUITMENT_DURATION_THEORY.md) against the registered [design](recruitment_duration_design.json), the [DASC observation decision](DASC_OBSERVATION_DECISION.md), and the retained [Myo1E findings](MYO1E_FINDINGS.md) and [repository assessment](MYO1E_REPOSITORY_FINDINGS.md). This is an analytic review, not an empirical fit or a biological mechanism claim.

**Disposition: accept as one bounded prospective falsification design; no present-data test.** The common conditional positivity function is defined only for eligible, selected observed tracks with total observed duration and a validated positive/negative denominator. It is a testable restriction of the selected joint distribution, whereas the earlier DASC result permits unrestricted condition-specific detection and selection. Rejection could establish a conditional observable difference under the declared measurement rules; acceptance would not identify a recruitment mechanism, show exogenous duration opportunity, or refute the DASC nonidentifiability.

The mathematics checks out. With total variation defined as `sup_A |F_1(A)-F_0(A)|`, layer cake gives `|p_1-p_0| <= TV` for any common measurable `0 <= g <= 1`; a Hahn-set indicator shows sharpness without a fixed baseline. At exact baseline `p_0=a`, the Lebesgue decomposition `F_1=ell F_0+F_{1,s}` yields the stated lower and upper integrals of the decreasing rearrangement of `ell`, with singular mass added only to the upper bound. Fractional values of `g` on a threshold level/atom supply exact baseline mass; values on `F_0`-null, `F_1`-positive support are free. Convexity fills the interval. The dual upper formula and `L(a)=1-U(1-a)` agree with this construction. The two-bin illustration is arithmetically correct: at `a=2/5`, the sharp interval is `[3/20,31/40]`, narrower than the coarse TV interval `[0,9/10]`.

The finite-bin formulation is correctly separated from the unrestricted continuous-duration null. Common `g(t)` can have different averages within the same bin if conditions populate different times there; the binned bound requires common *within-bin* positivity. The author applied one minor precision edit during review: prespecified bins now form a covering partition of the eligible duration domain up to both condition distributions' null sets, so the displayed sum includes every track. This does not change the result.

Practical calibration is still absent. Raw empirical TV for two finite continuous-duration samples may equal one simply because their observed support points differ; it is not a plug-in test threshold. A prospective check must freeze the eligible cohort, positivity rule, duration representation, and condition mapping, then carry uncertainty at independent movie/cell/culture units and account for censoring, selection, and pre/post pairing. Figure 4C's positive-to-negative ratio cannot be silently treated as a positive fraction. The retained 1,709-row three-color CSV has not validated the required positive and negative track cohort, codebook, denominator, condition mapping, or biological unit IDs. Therefore neither the published contrast nor the CSV currently supplies an evaluated inequality.

This design adds a useful *conditional observable* guard against reading a marginal Myo1E-positive change as altered recruitment. It is deliberately modest: total observed lifetime can be affected by recruitment and intervention, and conditioning on tracking retention and lifetime can induce collider or selection associations. The sharp envelope is a population feasibility criterion, not a causal duration model or a force result. The branch should stop at this reviewed design until the measurement contract is resolved; further theorem extensions would not resolve the missing cohort definitions.
