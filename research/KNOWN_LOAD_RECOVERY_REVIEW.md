# Independent review: fixed known load and one-profile recovery

Task `known-load-recovery-001`, attempt 1, 29 September 2026. I independently checked [the saved theory](KNOWN_LOAD_RECOVERY_THEORY.md) against the registered design, the exact source-flux calculation, the earlier unknown-load instability result, and the [lead's data comparison](KNOWN_LOAD_DATA_COMPARISON.md). **Disposition: accept the conditional theorem in its stated norms and model.** I find no algebraic or logical gap within its admitted stationary-profile range.

The sign and normalization are correct. For the full-square graph energy, with $t=u'+u/r-c$, $\lambda=\sigma/\kappa$, and $Q/\kappa=q$, stationarity gives $(rQ)'=-rf$. A regular origin excludes a $1/r$ integration term and fixes $q(r)=-(\kappa r)^{-1}\int_0^r sf(s)\,ds$. The exact flux is $q=-(1-u^2)t'-tuu'+(t^2/2+\lambda)u$; in particular, the Riccati coefficient and the $c^2$ contribution have the stated signs. Smooth compact support in $r\le1$ supplies $c(1)=0$, hence the terminal value $t(1)=u'(1)+u(1)$. Terminal-value ODE uniqueness then proves source uniqueness for the same exact profile without a small-amplitude assumption.

The near-flat estimate does not hide an assumed bound on the source. With $B=\|q\|_\infty$, $M=4(B+\lambda+1)$, and sufficiently small fixed $\delta_*$, inequality (8) gives a strict bound below $M$ on every backward interval where $|t|\le M$. A first-exit argument therefore bounds every compatible $t$ by $M$. On that bounded set the ODE is uniformly Lipschitz in $t,u,u'$; backward Gronwall controls $t_1-t_2$ by $\|u_1-u_2\|_{C^1[0,1]}$. Since $u_j(0)=0$, the quotient difference $(u_1-u_2)/r$ has the same control, including at the origin. Thus the stated $C^1$ normalized-slope to $C^0$ source bound has a constant depending on the **fixed** load, rigidity, tension, and support radius, but not on the profiles' distance to flatness. The source norm loses one radial derivative, as the reconstruction $c=u'+u/r-t$ requires.

The theorem correctly restricts recovery and stability to actual compatible stationary graphs. An arbitrary noisy $C^1$ slope can fail the exterior stationary equation, smooth source matching at $r=1$, and radial regularity. The estimate supplies no claim that such data have a compact-source explanation or that derivative errors are calibrated. Origin regularity and the prescribed load are indispensable: an unknown point reaction changes the flux constant, while the earlier unknown balanced-load construction changes $q$ and still gives an $O(\varepsilon)$ source change for only $O(\varepsilon^3)$ profile change. That construction is outside the fixed-load comparison.

The data comparison is appropriately limited. The retained LocMoFit cap summaries are neither a complete calibrated slope field with derivative uncertainty nor a verified single-valued stationary graph and reservoir; the recorded overhang fraction is consistent with the [observation contract](LOCMOFIT_OBSERVATION_CONTRACT.md). The [independent controls findings](INDEPENDENT_CONTROLS_FINDINGS.md) do not establish a known spatial pit-load field. Zero net force, a tube-force resultant, or an intervention label cannot be substituted for pointwise known $f(r)$. This analytic result therefore makes no empirical source estimate or mechanism selection from the assessed public tables. No new numerical or empirical work was needed for this review.
