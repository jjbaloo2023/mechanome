# Independent review: compact curvature-source uniqueness

**Finding.** For two stationary profiles of the *same* curvature source, vertical dead load and bending rigidity at distinct known tensions, the exact two-profile compatibility equation forces a smooth curvature-source change `g` supported in `0 <= r <= R0` to vanish if the sum of their normalized slopes is nonzero throughout `0 < r <= R0` and their normalized slopes differ on a dense subset of that interval. This is a sufficient, profile-conditional statement. It gives neither global source identifiability nor a stability estimate. The flat stationary family remains a counterexample to any unconditional claim.

Write `u_j = p_j/sqrt(1+p_j^2)`, `p_j=h_j'`, `D=u_1-u_2`, and `S=u_1+u_2`. Since `J_j^-2=1-u_j^2`, the registered coefficient satisfies `A=J_1^-2-J_2^-2=-(u_1-u_2)(u_1+u_2)=-DS`. The finite compatibility equation is therefore

`D[-S(rg)' + r(cg+g^2/2)] = 0`.

Where `D*S` is nonzero, `y=rg` obeys the finite Riccati equation `y'=(c/S)y + y^2/(2rS)`. Linearization at `g=0` gives `y'=(c/S)y`. The quadratic term matters: first-order compatibility is not a complete finite-source test.

For the stated sufficient criterion, continuity extends the divided equation across isolated zeros of `D`, because `D` is nonzero on a dense set and `S` remains nonzero. Smooth zero extension of the compact source gives `y(R0)=0`. At this positive-radius endpoint the right-hand side is locally Lipschitz in `y`; uniqueness of the initial-value problem yields `y=0` on each connected inward interval and hence throughout `(0,R0]`. One may propagate through any compact interval `[epsilon,R0]`; the regular radial origin need not have a nonzero slope sum. Continuity gives `g(0)=0`, and radial regularity (`g'(0)=0`) excludes a point-source interpretation. The `S(R0) != 0` boundary condition is substantive: a zero of `S` at the only known zero-data endpoint makes this particular propagation proof singular.

The degeneracies have different meanings. Because `p/sqrt(1+p^2)` is strictly increasing, `D=0` means equal slopes. At a single such radius the equation is vacuous, but dense neighboring `D != 0` still supplies the divided equation by continuity. On an open equal-slope interval, the displayed source-change equation imposes no constraint on `g` supported inside that interval. For actual common-load profiles regular at the origin, however, the global stationary flux equality forces that common slope to be zero throughout the interval. `S=0` means opposite slopes. If `D != 0` there, finite compatibility reduces to `g(c+g/2)=0`, with pointwise branches `g=0` and `g=-2c`; infinitesimal compatibility is only `cg=0`. An isolated opposite-slope zero makes the Riccati coefficient singular and cannot be crossed by the above uniqueness argument without extra regularity or sign information. If both slopes vanish, `D=S=0` and the compatibility equation is vacuous there.

An actual stationary nonuniqueness example is the flat family: with `h_1=h_2=0` and `f=-kappa Delta_r c`, any smooth compact radial `g`, together with `delta f=-kappa Delta_r g`, retains the same flat profiles at both tensions and has balanced added load. This establishes a real failure of unrestricted-source uniqueness, not merely an algebraic coefficient choice.

Even when the sufficient criterion holds, inversion can be poorly conditioned. Small `|D|` suppresses the original compatibility residual, while small `|S|` amplifies the divided Riccati coefficients. In the joint small-slope, small-source regime the finite gauge-breaking terms enter beyond the leading linear theory. Exact uniqueness under the criterion therefore does not imply useful precision from noisy profile measurements. Any empirical application must first verify the profile and source-support prerequisites under calibrated load, coordinate and boundary conventions.

## Independent cross-check against companion derivations

[The theory derivation](NONLINEAR_UNIQUENESS_THEORY.md) obtains the same factorization, finite and infinitesimal equations, and sufficient boundary-propagation theorem. Its additional origin expansion is consistent: for `p_j=a_j r+O(r^3)` with `a_1 != a_2`, an origin value `g_0` must satisfy `g_0[c_0+g_0/2-(a_1+a_2)]=0`; that is a local necessary value, not a compact global branch. No correction to the theorem is needed.

[The actual-equilibrium check](NONLINEAR_UNIQUENESS_COMPATIBILITY.md) sharpens the equal-slope exception. Because both stationary profiles have the same `f`, `(r(Q_1-Q_2))'=0` globally. Regularity at the origin sets the integration constant to zero, so `Q_1=Q_2` everywhere. On any equal-slope open interval all bending terms in the flux match, leaving `(sigma_1-sigma_2)p/J=0`; distinct tensions force `p=0` there, even on an annulus. A nonzero catenoid segment solves only the weaker locally integrated differential equation and cannot match the global regular-origin flux equality. For opposite-slope intervals the companion flux relation likewise has zero integration constant; the algebraic finite branch `g=-2c` alone does not show actual joint equilibrium. These necessary conditions and the flat family delimit the current result without proving that a nonflat pair satisfies the uniqueness hypotheses.

The most direct later gate is existence of **actual** common-source stationary profiles whose normalized slope sum stays nonzero through the declared source-support boundary and whose slope difference has no open zero interval. A small compact curvature forcing at two fixed positive tensions is a plausible separately registered analytic test, but branch existence, strict ordering and boundary behavior remain unproved here. Algebraic test curves or a chosen numerical epsilon cannot substitute for those checks.
