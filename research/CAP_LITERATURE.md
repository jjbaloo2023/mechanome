# Spherical-cap literature boundary and novelty disposition

**Scope.** This note compares the spherical-cap energy used by
`curvo/evaluator_tier0.py::_cap_energy` and the default curvature trajectory in
`curvo/inverse.py`.  It is a source check, not a claim that the reduced model
captures endocytosis or membrane-domain dynamics.  Source access was checked on
2026-09-22 (America/New_York); this is an observation time, not a reconstructed
research start time.

## What the repository model actually assumes

For a cap of fixed surface area `A`, opening angle `psi`, and sphere radius
`R = [A/(2 pi (1-cos psi))]^(1/2)`, the implementation minimizes

`E = (kappa/2) (2/R-c0)^2 A + lambda 2 pi R sin(psi)
     + sigma [A-pi(R sin(psi))^2] - F R(1-cos(psi))/kBT`.

The cap is circular and spherical at every frame, its coated area is held fixed,
and `sigma` is a single scalar footprint penalty.  The edge term is a *domain
line tension*, whereas the code calls `sigma` membrane tension.  The active term
is prescribed axial work, not a force balance generated by a network.  The
inference default is 24 frames with a shared coverage ramp: increasing effective
spontaneous curvature and active force, increasing coat rigidity, fixed `A`, and
**line tension fixed to zero** (`curvo/inverse.py`, `_fast_H_trajectory`).

At `c0=F=0`, define `x=1-cos(psi)` in `[0,2]`.  The implemented energy reduces,
up to an additive constant, to

`E(x) = (4 pi kappa + sigma A/2)x + lambda sqrt(2 pi A (2-x))`.

Thus with `lambda=0` and nonnegative `kappa,sigma`, it is monotone increasing:
the flat endpoint is the sole minimizer and there is no interior metastable cap
or snap-through barrier in that restricted energy.  This is an algebraic result
for the repository ansatz, not a general statement about membranes.  Nonzero
`c0` or active work can deform the cap, but do not reinstate the missing free
boundary, surrounding-membrane, or neck mechanics.

### Geometry and boundary conditions that matter

Lipowsky's domain construction and the repository share the spherical-cap
reduction, but the former is a phase/domain boundary problem.  Hassinger instead
keeps the surrounding membrane, lets local tension vary under heterogeneous
spontaneous curvature/force, solves a spatial shape equation, and stops near a
5-nm neck separation (arXiv HTML lines 89–95).  The repository collapses all of
that to a spherical cap with an imposed footprint and no neck radius or
surrounding shape.  Akamatsu adds nonequilibrium filament nucleation, growth,
bending, and attachment; force is an output of network dynamics and adapts to
load.  Consequently, a cap optimum can be used only as a coarse geometric
readout, not as evidence of scission, a physical transition time, or a uniquely
measured actin force.

## Candidate rigidity-invariant force/tension ridge

The candidate supplied for review is, under its stated zero-edge-line-tension,
fixed-area, shared-ramp reduction,

`H = u [4 pi kappa(t) C + Fmax/kBT] / [8 pi kappa(t) + sigma A]`.

If `Fmax/kBT = C sigma A/2`, then `H = C u/2` for every `kappa(t)`: changing
coat rigidity does not identify force or tension along that exact compensation
condition.  This is a useful **model-specific algebraic identifiability result**
to test against the code's numerical argmin and units.  It is not a novelty
claim.

The checked primary papers establish related but weaker points: Hassinger's
dimensionless vesiculation number, `Ves=(C0/2)sqrt(kappa/lambda)` (arXiv Eq./text
at lines 111–117), combines curvature, bending rigidity, and membrane tension;
Akamatsu explicitly contrasts an adaptive force-generating network with a fixed
energy load relation.  Neither checked source states this exact force–tension
compensation equation, rigidity-invariant curvature trajectory, or its
identifiability consequence.  The bounded disposition is therefore **unresolved
as to literature novelty**: it may be a direct consequence of the reduced cap
algebra, and the absence of it in this deliberately small primary-source check is
not evidence of novelty.

## A test that would extend rather than rederive

Add an independently measured neck/base actin observable, following Akamatsu's
base/neck localization concept, and fit it jointly with curvature.  The
network-specific prediction that this observable breaks the cap ridge remains a
testable hypothesis and requires an explicit network forward model; it is not
established by the indexed excerpt.  For a concrete within-cap test, perturb
coat area while holding the shared parameters fixed: rigidity variation alone
cannot break the stated exact ridge, whereas the proposed ridge condition depends
on `A`.  A neck-radius or full-contour measurement is required to test for the
Hassinger-type full-shape branches; a cap-summary curvature by itself does not
provide that full-shape observable.
