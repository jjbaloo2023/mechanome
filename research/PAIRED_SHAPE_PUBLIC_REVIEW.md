# Independent review: paired shape/coat public screen

## Registered scope and decision rule

This review uses the saved source and lead evidence only. The registered question requires a genuinely new, reusable public object containing time-resolved **membrane orientation or shape** and coat signal for the same individual clathrin events. A source-data listing or a paper-level example is not enough: the object/file identity and accessible listing, event-level pairing, time calibration/registration, selection/censoring, and cell/preparation grouping must be established or left as exact gaps. An axial clathrin proxy can be considered only with its stated optical and biological interpretation, not silently relabelled direct membrane shape. The screen excludes the previously assessed records named in `paired_shape_public_design.json` and permits no additional reviewer source calls.

## One candidate and disposition

The bounded screen found a genuinely new paper-level candidate: Nawara et al., *Nature Communications* 13:1732 (2022), DOI `10.1038/s41467-022-29317-1`. The primary article describes simultaneously acquired two-color signals from dual-tagged CLCa, an intensity time course and a ratio-calibrated axial-distribution proxy on individual tracks. The publisher page lists public Source Data as an XLSX. The workbook body, its exact sheet schema, individual-event time series/IDs, and a cell/repeat map were not inspected. Thus the verified object is an article-level public workbook listing, **not yet an analysis-ready paired event dataset**. It is distinct from the excluded prior records.

The axial ratio reports the distribution of the clathrin probe. Its interpretation as membrane shape/curvature is indirect and conditional on the paper's optical and biological assumptions; it is not an independent membrane orientation or morphology channel. This is a useful same-track **coat intensity plus coat axial-position proxy** candidate. The design permits scrutiny of optical proxies, so lack of a separate membrane label is not by itself a newly invented exclusion rule; any later use must name the proxy rather than claim direct membrane shape. A schema check could establish paired event rows but would not independently validate the membrane-shape interpretation. Neither a flexible-coat mechanism, force, nor scission follows from this screen.

The article states simultaneous channels, bead-based axial calibration, first-ten-frame normalization, threshold and five-consecutive-image rules, exclusion of pre-existing and >100-second tracks, and nested event/cell/repeat counts. These are valuable contract clues, not a file-level eligibility or grouping map. The exact per-event cadence/registration fields, censoring labels, cell and repeat IDs, and accessible paired rows remain unverified in the workbook. In particular, 1,948 tracks are nested in 13 cells and five repeats, and the smaller figure cohort is not interchangeable with the full cohort; tracks cannot be treated as independent biological replicates.

The source used two searches and two primary opens; the lead used one primary open, for the registered total of two searches and three opens. No reviewer source calls, raw acquisition, code, fits, or tracking occurred. The lead independently verified the primary modality and did not inspect the workbook. This is a bounded exact gap, not evidence that no suitable public dataset exists. The current screen should stop here; a later, separately registered small workbook-schema check would address only the narrower axial-proxy question if that question becomes a priority.
