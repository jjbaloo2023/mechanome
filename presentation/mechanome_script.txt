[1] REAL VIDEO - FLU ENTERING A CELL
This is real. An actual flu virus getting into a living cell.
Green and red are the proteins that pull it in.
You can see them show up -
but that doesn't tell you what makes the cell commit to swallowing it.

[2] THE REAL QUESTION
What decides it is force. And you can't see force in a movie.
So we need a model that says which proteins -
and which parts of them - do the pulling.

[3] WHAT MECHANOME DOES
That's Mechanome. It goes both ways.
From a protein's shape, it predicts the physics.
From real images, it works back to the force.
And if the data can't pin the force down, it says so - it won't guess.

[4] THE RULE IT FOUND
Here's what it found. Build the pit up one piece at a time.
One protein alone - nothing. Add force alone - still nothing.
It only tips over the edge when both come together.
Neither one is enough by itself.

[5] WATCH IT RUN
Watch it work. Same movie, same engine.
With the full data, it gives a clear number.
Take away one piece, and it can't tell - so it stops.

[6] TWO ANSWERS
Forty-one - and the real answer was forty. Spot on.
Or: I don't know - and here's the experiment that would tell you.
It only gives a number when the data can back it up.

[7] IT HOLDS UP
And it's real. The same pattern shows up in three different cell types.
And it gets measured forces right, to within four percent.

[8] BACK TO THE VIRUS
So - the flu virus. It needs one specific protein, epsin, to bend the membrane.
Take that part away, and the virus can't get in.
Move that part onto another protein, and the power moves with it.

[9] IT'S OPEN
All of it is open. The code, the paper, everything. Anyone can check it.

[10] THE TAKEAWAY
So: read a protein's job from its shape.
Find the rule for what commits a pit.
And show which protein a virus hijacks to get in.

[11] GIVE IT TO CLAUDE
And since it just takes a movie and a question, you can hand it to Claude -
and let it work through problems like this on its own:
an answer when it can, the next experiment when it can't.
